Qiyam & Fajr Alarm

An iPhone alarm that follows the prayer times.

Support

Found a bug, or a time that looks wrong? Open an issue on GitHub and include your city and the method shown in the app's Settings. Posting needs a free GitHub account.

An alarm didn't ring

Open your iPhone's Settings, go to Apps, then Qiyam & Fajr, and check Alarms is on. Then tap Test Alarm in the app's Settings to hear one. Alarms won't ring if the app is locked or hidden with Face ID, because iOS doesn't allow it.

The times differ from my mosque

Mosques use different calculation methods. Try another one under Method in the app's Settings. Fajr can move by 15 minutes or more between methods.

I travelled to another city

Open the app once when you arrive. It updates the location and moves your alarms to match.

Do I need to keep opening the app?

No. Alarms are scheduled two weeks ahead and extend each time you stop one. Opening the app also extends them.

Privacy policy

Last updated 31 October 2026

Qiyam & Fajr Alarm collects no personal data. There are no accounts, ads, analytics or trackers, and nothing from your iPhone reaches the developer.

Location

With your permission, the app reads your approximate location to work out prayer times. It is saved only on your iPhone. You can type a city instead of sharing your location.

To show your city's name, or find a city you search for, your iPhone asks Apple Maps. Apple's privacy policy covers those lookups.

Alarms and settings

These are saved only on your iPhone and are removed when you delete the app.

Children

The app collects nothing from anyone, including children.

Changes and contact

If this policy ever changes, this page will be updated. Questions are welcome on GitHub.
